# Assessment findings and limitations

Assessment progress — 16 October 2026
This assessment examines recorded Alcedo atthis sightings in the Netherlands from 2010–2025. Question: how do sighting counts vary over months and years? The source has 429,646 records and 50 variables. A derived monthly table contains 192 calendar months and 429,619 records; 27 records without a month remain in yearly totals. sighting_count counts occurrence records, not distinct birds or population size. The variable dictionary describes the original dataset; the charts and relationship analysis use monthly sighting counts. Group roles and final review remain for the group.

Parquet is a structured tabular dataset. All columns were preserved as text on export; classifications below refer to meaning, not physical storage. Empty source fields became NULL. R may display these as NA.

Classification conventions: numeric identifiers remain nominal. taxonRank is treated as an ordered hierarchy. month is cyclic, so a numerical average is only a descriptive summary. Alphabetic permits spaces and separators; alphanumeric means mixed text. Fully empty fields are classified by intended meaning. No genuine Boolean field is present.

Verified source: 429,646 records, 50 columns, 21,482,300 cells and 7,265,072 NULL cells (33.82%). Exact duplicate rows and repeated gbifID values are both zero; separate records can still describe the same real-world event. There are 11 contributing datasets. For monthly analysis, 429,619 records have valid year/month values; 27 lack a month. Aggregation produces 192 complete monthly count rows, January 2010–December 2025.
Source: GBIF occurrence download, DOI https://doi.org/10.15468/dl.5kx93n (18 March 2026). Its verified filters are HUMAN_OBSERVATION, country NL, status PRESENT and years 2010–2025. The original-field summaries above describe the source data. The primary derived variable for the following analysis is sighting_count: the number of records in each year-month. Across 192 months, minimum=209 (June 2012), maximum=6,296 (January 2021), median=1,979.5, mean=2,237.60 sightings/month. Counts are numeric, discrete, non-negative integers; year_month is a calendar period. These derived variables do not change the source's 50-column count.

sighting_count is COUNT(*) grouped by year and month. There are 192 monthly observations with no missing counts. The histogram uses 16 bins; its local peaks depend on binning, so strict unimodality is not claimed. Positive skewness (+0.6658) reflects a longer tail of high-count months. These are recorded sightings, not numbers of unique birds.

Quartiles use linear interpolation; fractional quartiles are valid summaries of integer counts. The fourth entry is the 100th percentile (maximum). Standard deviation uses n−1. Units are sightings per calendar month; the sample is 192 months, not 429,646 individual records.

The 1.5×IQR fences are −2,224.125 and 6,368.875 sightings/month. All monthly counts (209–6,296) fall inside these fences, so there are no flagged monthly outliers. The negative lower fence is a statistical threshold, not a possible count. High-count months are retained. This result replaces the earlier coordinate-based outlier analysis.

For 192 months, time_index runs from 0 (January 2010) to 191 (December 2025). Sample covariance(time_index, sighting_count)=54,850.4267 month-index × sightings/month. Pearson r=0.6737 and Spearman rho=0.7128 show a positive temporal association, with pronounced fluctuations rather than a strictly monotonic increase. Annual totals rise from 6,972 records in 2010 to 50,153 in 2025, with falls in intervening years. Annual totals include the 27 records without a month. This describes reporting patterns, not population growth.

All correlations use sighting_count as the response on the same 192 monthly rows. month_sin=sin(2π×month/12) and month_cos=cos(2π×month/12) encode the calendar cycle together; they are not independent causal factors. year and time_index are alternative time descriptors, not two independent findings. Strength bands are descriptive: |r|<0.1 negligible, <0.3 weak, <0.5 moderate, otherwise strong. Record-level coordinates and individualCount are not correlated directly with aggregate counts because their rows have a different unit of observation. Serial dependence and unequal month lengths limit inference; no causal or significance claim is made. Reproduce with assessment/analyze_sightings.py.

This is a proposed conceptual model for the occurrence-record system. Physical denotes the measured location/count property; informational denotes identifiers, classifications, metadata or descriptions. Continuant denotes an enduring entity; occurrent denotes an observation, identification or processing event. A property recorded about an event is marked occurrent. Alternative ontology choices should be agreed consistently by the group.
